How to Read Inch Sizes

1. Diameter : 35

35 inches

The overall outer height of this tyre is 35 inches from tread to tread when inflated. In a high-flotation size, the first number is always the tyre's total standing diameter.

2. Section Width : 12.50

12.5 inches

The cross-section width of this tyre is 12.5 inches from the widest point of the inner sidewall to the outer sidewall.

3. Tire Construction : R

R (Radial)

The letter R stands for radial ply construction, where internal casing cords run straight across the tyre. Radial is the standard construction method used on almost all modern tyres.

4. Rim / Wheel Diameter : 15

15 inches

The wheel diameter is 15 inches. This tyre is built specifically to fit a 15-inch wheel rim and must not be mounted on any other rim size.

5. Tire Class : LT

LT (Light Truck)

The designation LT identifies this as a Light Truck tyre, engineered with stronger sidewalls and higher load capacity than standard passenger tyres.

6. Load Range : E

E (10-ply rating)

Load range E corresponds to a 10-ply strength rating (up to 80 psi). Common LT load ranges include B (4-ply), C (6-ply), D (8-ply), E (10-ply), and F (12-ply).

1. Tire Class : P

Intended Vehicle Type

The optional letter at the very start of a metric tyre size identifies the service class of vehicle the tyre is built for:

P (Passenger): Stands for passenger vehicle tyres used on everyday cars, SUVs, crossovers, minivans, and light-duty pickups.

LT (Light Truck): Designed for vehicles carrying heavy payloads, towing trailers, or operating off-road, such as 3/4-ton and 1-ton pickups and heavy SUVs.

ST (Special Trailer): Built specifically for trailer axles (travel, boat, fifth-wheel, and utility trailers) with stiffer sidewalls that reduce trailer sway.

No Prefix (Euro-Metric): When there is no letter before the first number (for example, 205/55R16), it is a European/ISO metric size. It measures width in millimetres the same way, though its rated load capacity at a given pressure may differ slightly from a P-metric equivalent.

2. Section Width : 255

Measured in Millimetres

The width of this tyre is 255 mm across its widest points from inner sidewall to outer sidewall when mounted on a standard wheel. Always convert millimetres to inches by dividing by 25.4 (255 ÷ 25.4 = 10.04 inches).

3. Aspect Ratio : 60

Sidewall Height Percentage

The number after the slash is not a direct measurement — it is a percentage of the section width indicating how tall the sidewall is from rim flange to tread. On a 255/60 tyre, the sidewall height is 60% of 255 mm = 153 mm (about 6.02 inches). Lower numbers (such as 40 or 45) give shorter, sportier sidewalls; higher numbers (such as 70 or 75) give taller sidewalls for extra cushioning.

4. Tire Construction : R

Internal Carcass Layer

The letter R stands for Radial construction, in which internal ply cords run at 90 degrees across the tread direction. Almost all modern road tyres use radial construction (older bias-ply tyres may use D or -).

5. Rim Diameter : 17

Wheel Fitment in Inches

Even on a metric tyre, the wheel rim diameter is still measured in inches. A tyre marked 17 must be fitted only onto a 17-inch rim.

6. Load Index : 102

Weight Capacity Code

The 2- or 3-digit number at the start of the service description corresponds to the maximum weight one tyre can safely carry at maximum inflation pressure. Here, 102 equals 1,874 lbs (850 kg) per tyre (view full Load Ratings chart). Replacement tyres should match or exceed your vehicle manufacturer's required load index.

7. Speed Rating : V

Sustained Speed Limit

The final letter indicates the highest sustained highway speed the tyre is certified to maintain under its rated load. A V rating certifies the tyre up to 149 mph (240 km/h) (view full Speed Ratings chart).

Part 03

Converting Metric to Inches

There are two ways that tyre manufacturers display tyre size: inches and metric. Reading a tyre size expressed directly in inches is very straightforward.

Example A : 33X12.50R15

The first number is your overall diameter of the tyre. In this example the tyre diameter or "height" is 33 inches tall. The second number represents the width of the tyre. In most cases with inch measurement tyres the width will have a .50 decimal after the initial number. In Example "A" this reads 12.50, meaning the tyre width is 12 and 1/2 inches. The final number simply is the wheel size or diameter of the wheel that the tyre will fit on (15 inches).

Reading a tyre size with a metric measurement takes one extra calculation step because width is given in millimetres and sidewall height is given as a percentage.

Example B : 285/75R16

On metric tyres the first number is your width of the tyre in millimetres. In Example "B" this reads 285 mm. Since there are 25.4 mm in one inch, you divide 285 by 25.4 to get 11.22" — so the width of a 285 mm tyre is 11.22 inches. The next number (75) represents the percentage of the width that forms the sidewall height: 75% of 11.22" equals 8.41" (or 214 mm). Because a tyre has two sidewalls (top and bottom around the wheel), you multiply one sidewall by 2 and add the 16" wheel diameter: (8.41 × 2) = 16.82" plus 16" gives an overall height of 32.82". Therefore, a 285/75R16 metric tyre converts to an inch size of 32.82X11.22R16.

Quick Formula: Overall Diameter (in) = [(Width mm × Aspect Ratio ÷ 2,540) × 2] + Rim Diameter (in). Remember, not all tyre sizes are manufactured in both metric and inch formats — you can also verify any conversion instantly with our Tyre Size Calculator →.
